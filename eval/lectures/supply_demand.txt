Supply and demand determine prices in a competitive market. The demand curve shows how much of a good consumers are willing to buy at each price. As price rises, quantity demanded usually falls, which is called the law of demand. The supply curve shows how much producers are willing to sell at each price. Market equilibrium occurs where the supply curve and the demand curve intersect. At the equilibrium price, quantity supplied equals quantity demanded. A price ceiling set below equilibrium creates a shortage, while a price floor set above equilibrium creates a surplus. Rent control is a common example of a price ceiling, and the minimum wage is a common example of a price floor. Price elasticity of demand measures how strongly quantity demanded responds to a change in price. Goods with many substitutes tend to have elastic demand. Necessities such as insulin tend to have inelastic demand because buyers cannot easily go without them. An increase in consumer income shifts the demand curve to the right for normal goods. For inferior goods, demand falls when income rises. A tax on producers shifts the supply curve to the left and raises the equilibrium price.
